# Torah Law categories

> This page records the original category pass. For current totals, the corrected workbook and the record-by-record decisions, see the [independent Torah Law category audit](INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md).

**All 646 Torah Law records now carry two study labels: a form from the Jewish division of the commandments, and a category from the traditional Christian division of the Mosaic law.** 334 are positive, 293 negative and 19 permissions. 134 are moral, 312 ceremonial and 200 civil. 112 records are flagged below for your decision.

The labels sit in their own data file beside the catalogue. The site's classification field, which says how each law applies to Christians today, is unchanged.

## What the labels mean

| Label | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form (Jewish tradition) | Positive | Requires an action, a procedure, or a status or penalty to be applied (mitzvat aseh). |
| Form (Jewish tradition) | Negative | Forbids an action (mitzvat lo ta'aseh). |
| Form (Jewish tradition) | Permission | Allows an action without requiring it. |
| Category (traditional Christian division) | Moral | A duty of love toward God or neighbour, stated as right or wrong in itself. |
| Category (traditional Christian division) | Ceremonial | A rite or holiness rule of Israel's worship under the old covenant. |
| Category (traditional Christian division) | Civil | A rule for Israel as a nation: its courts, penalties, government, land and economy. |
| Ceremonial area | Sacrifice and offerings | Sacrifices, offerings, tithes, firstfruits and firstborn animals for the altar. |
| Ceremonial area | Priesthood and sanctuary | Priests, Levites, the high priest, the tabernacle and its holy things. |
| Ceremonial area | Dietary laws | Clean and unclean animals, blood, fat, carcasses and other food prohibitions. |
| Ceremonial area | Ritual purity | Impurity and cleansing: corpses, skin disease, discharges, childbirth. |
| Ceremonial area | Festivals and holy days | Sabbath rites, new moons, the annual feasts, and the sacred side of the sabbatical year and Jubilee. |
| Ceremonial area | Signs, vows and dedications | Circumcision, tassels, mixed kinds, vows, Nazirites, valuations and devoted things. |

A record can have a second category when it clearly has two characters. The weekly Sabbath, for example, is moral as one of the Ten Commandments and ceremonial in its day-keeping. When a provision holds both a requirement and a prohibition, its form is that of its main clause, and it is marked as mixed.

Form is judged from the operative words of the Saints Translation verse, not from the record's title. Following Maimonides, a court's duty to carry out a penalty and a rule that sets a status (such as an impurity period) count as positive.

### Compared with the traditional 613

Maimonides counts 248 positive and 365 negative commandments, so 40% are positive. In this catalogue 52% are positive, because it itemizes many procedures, penalties and status rules, which count as positive, and lists permissions separately.

478 records were matched to Maimonides' commandments in the Torah Law count check. Of the 424 matched only to positive or only to negative commandments, 406 take the same form here; the others are flagged below.

## How the labels relate to the site's classification

The classification field is left as it is. The site's legend keeps "Israel’s civil and land laws", "Covenant-specific instructions" and the historical categories so that these laws "should not be forced into one of the four Christian-application marks". They are statements about application, not a mix-up of kinds, so the new category sits beside them rather than replacing them.

## Accuracy check

A separate reviewer classified 63 records (nine records at random from each of the seven review batches) from the same brief, without seeing the reviewers' answers. The two agreed on the form of all 63 records and on the main category of 62; the one difference (C0931) swaps the main and second categories and was already flagged. They agreed on the second category for 58, on whether the form is mixed for 61, and on the ceremonial area for 26 of the 27 both called ceremonial. No answer was changed as a result.

## How it was done

1. **Inputs.** Each of the 646 records was given with its Saints Translation verse text, its topic, the site's classification, its original audience and the Maimonides commandments matched to it in the Torah Law count check.
2. **Review.** Seven reviewers each took about 93 records and assigned form, category, any second category and ceremonial area from the verse, following [the brief](method/brief.md). Each output passed [a validator](scripts/validate_cat.py).
3. **Cross-checks.** Forms were compared with the 613 matches and categories with the site's classification, and recurring kinds of law (penalties, food laws, permissions, the Ten Commandments, the Sabbath) were compared across the seven batches. Records that differ, and anything below high confidence, are flagged.
4. **Accuracy check.** A separate reviewer re-checked a random sample.

## Limits

- Both schemes are interpretive traditions, not labels Scripture supplies. The moral, ceremonial and civil division follows Thomas Aquinas and the Westminster Confession (chapter 19); dispensational and Messianic readers use it differently.
- Forms rest on the Saints Translation wording. Jewish enumerators also differ on some edge cases.
- The labels were drafted by reviewers and cross-checked, not decided by the site's editors. The flagged records need your decision.
