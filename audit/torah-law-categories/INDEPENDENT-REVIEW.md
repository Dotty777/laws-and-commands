# Independent Torah Law category audit

Reviewed all **646** records in the category workbook against the archived Saints Translation exports. The review covered Exodus (132 records), Leviticus (270), Numbers (79), and Deuteronomy (165). The supplied workbook matched the GitHub workbook byte for byte before the audit. Its 646 category rows matched the published category layer.

The audit corrected **47 records**, including **37 with changes to a form, mixed flag, category, second category or ceremonial area**. It also recorded 33 interpretive judgments, including three confidence adjustments, and six source qualifications. The workbook flags 115 records for reasons that include confidence flags and differences from Maimonides or the site’s separate application classification.

[Download the audited workbook](Saints-Bible-Torah-Law-Categories-2026-11-08.xlsx) · [Browse Torah Law](../../browse/torah-law/README.md) · [Category data](../../data/current/torah-law-categories.json)

## How the review was conducted

- The original passage and the scope of each catalogue entry determine which provision the form describes. A mixed label requires both forms within the same entry. Reviewers treated provisions for different audiences, conditions, and separately indexed duties according to their own scope.
- The Moral, Ceremonial and Civil assignments follow the repository’s editorial framework. The text decides the direct subject of each rule. Its Israelite ritual or legal setting can support a second category.
- A stated consequence establishes a status under the Positive convention. A Civil court classification requires evidence that the rule concerns judicial enforcement.
- Maimonides’ numbering provides a comparison. The review distinguishes his derivation from the operative words of the Saints Translation. Permission is an additional catalogue label for what a provision allows.

Sources include the [Saints Translation exports](../../source/translation/books/), [the original rubric](method/brief.md), [Aquinas on the Old Law](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2099.htm), [Westminster Confession, chapter 19](https://opc.org/wcf.html#Chapter_19), and [Maimonides’ Sefer HaMitzvot](https://www.sefaria.org/Sefer_HaMitzvot). Aquinas discusses the assembly as public standing in [Summa I–II, question 105, article 3](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/2105.htm#article3). Maimonides discusses its marriage restrictions in [Forbidden Intercourse, chapter 12](https://new.mechon-mamre.org/i/en/5112.htm).
