Torah: Leviticus 5:4–6

Leviticus addresses a rashly spoken oath, recognition of guilt, confession and the prescribed offering. It does not teach that a harmful promise must be carried out merely because it was spoken. Jesus and James call for dependable, truthful speech; repentance must not become an excuse to fulfill an evil intention.

> And the Lord spoke unto Moses, saying:
> Speak unto the sons of Israel, and you shall say unto them: If a man or woman solemnly vows a vow of consecration unto the Lord, to be set apart
> from wine and strong drink, he shall be purified from wine. He shall drink neither vinegar of wine, nor vinegar of strong drink, nor anything prepared from grapes; and he shall eat neither fresh grapes nor raisins.
> All the days of his vow he shall consume nothing made from the vine: wine, from pressed grape skins unto the seed.
> All the days of the vow of purification no razor shall come upon his head. Until the days which he vowed unto the Lord are fulfilled, he shall be holy, letting the hair of his head grow long.
> All the days of his vow unto the Lord he shall not enter where any dead person is.
> For his father, or his mother, or his brother, or his sister, he shall not defile himself when they die; for the vow of his God is upon him, upon his head.
> All the days of his vow he shall be holy unto the Lord.
> But if anyone dies suddenly beside him, the head of his vow shall be defiled forthwith. He shall shave his head on the day when he is cleansed; on the seventh day it shall be shaved.
> And on the eighth day he shall bring two turtledoves or two young pigeons unto the priest, at the doors of the Tent of Testimony.
> And the priest shall prepare one for sin and one for a whole burnt offering. And the priest shall make atonement for him concerning his sin in connection with the dead person, and shall consecrate his head on that day,
> whereon he was consecrated unto the Lord for the days of the vow. And he shall bring a year-old lamb for a guilt offering; and the former days shall not be reckoned, for the head of his vow was defiled.
> And this is the law of him who has vowed: on the day when he fulfils the days of his vow, he shall present his offering at the doors of the Tent of Testimony,
> and he shall bring his gift unto the Lord: one unblemished year-old male lamb for whole burning, and one unblemished year-old ewe lamb for sin, and one unblemished ram for deliverance,
> and a basket of unleavened bread of fine flour, loaves prepared with oil, and unleavened wafers spread with oil, and their sacrifice and their drink offering.
> And the priest shall present them before the Lord, and prepare his offering for sin and his whole burnt offering.
> And he shall prepare the ram as a sacrifice of deliverance unto the Lord, with the basket of unleavened bread; and the priest shall prepare its sacrifice and its drink offering.
> And he who has vowed shall shave the head of his vow at the doors of the Tent of Testimony, and put the hair upon the fire which is under the sacrifice of deliverance.
> And the priest shall take the boiled foreleg from the ram, and one unleavened loaf from the basket, and one unleavened wafer, and put them upon the hands of him who has vowed, after he has shaved the hair of his vow.
> And the priest shall bring them as a presentation before the Lord. It shall be holy unto the priest, with the breast of presentation and the foreleg of the portion set apart; and afterward he who has vowed shall drink wine.
> This is the law of him who vows unto the Lord, his gift unto the Lord concerning the vow, apart from whatever his hand may find, according to the extent of his vow which he vows under the law of consecration.
