Study 12: Circumcision in Abraham’s covenant

Related indexed requirements: C0036. These links indicate overlapping passages; they do not assert that every requirement is identical to the study’s subject.

Transformed — covenant belonging and circumcision

Connection to Christ: Acts and Galatians explicitly reject requiring circumcision for Gentile salvation.

Christian response today: Belong to Christ in faith, receive his call to baptism and live in love; do not require physical circumcision for salvation or condemn someone for their existing circumcised or uncircumcised status.

Saints Translation — Law: Genesis 17:10–14 Christ and the apostles: Acts 15:1–11 · Galatians 5:2–6 · 1 Corinthians 7:18–19

Ancient covenant practice: Circumcision marks Abraham’s male descendants and household within the covenant.

Development in Christ and the apostles: Acts rejects circumcision as a requirement for Gentile salvation. Paul describes circumcision of the heart, a circumcision not made by hands, and burial and resurrection with Christ in baptism. Faith working through love is decisive.

What Transformed means · Browse transformed practices

Circumcision on the eighth day is the covenant sign commanded for Abraham’s male descendants and household. The New Testament explicitly addresses its application to Gentiles: it must not be required for salvation or treated as the basis of righteousness. The historical command remains Scripture without becoming a compulsory Christian initiation rite.
